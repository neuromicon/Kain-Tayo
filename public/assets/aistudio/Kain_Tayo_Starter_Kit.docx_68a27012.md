Organizer Handbook • 30-Day Launch Guide • Vendor Agreement

A practical, locally operated model for funding free rice servings through existing food vendors. Adapt the city-specific fields before use.

# **1\. The Kain Tayo model**

Kain Tayo is a decentralized, community-supported food initiative. It does not build kitchens, store cooked food, operate restaurants, or run delivery. It works through existing local food vendors using BUY 1 \+ GIVE 1\.

1. A customer pays for two identical rice servings at the vendor’s normal price.  
2. The customer receives one serving immediately.  
3. The second serving is recorded as a prepaid credit in that vendor’s Rice Bank.  
4. Another person can claim that serving free of charge from the same vendor.  
5. The vendor receives the full payment for both servings at the time of purchase.

Example: if one serving normally costs ₱12, the customer pays ₱24, receives one serving, and one prepaid serving is added to that vendor’s Rice Bank. No donation-processing fee is charged.

# **2\. Non-negotiable principles**

* Access is open to everyone. Priority may be given to students, schoolchildren, and children from financially challenged households.  
* No beneficiary registration, mandatory ID, income verification, or collection of beneficiary names or personal details.  
* No questions, judgment, public labeling, or photography as a condition of access.  
* Each participating vendor maintains a separate Rice Bank.  
* In Version 1, a prepaid serving is redeemable only at the same vendor where it was purchased.  
* Prepaid servings and actually redeemed servings are reported separately.  
* No credit without corresponding payment; no double counting.  
* Administrative expenses are accounted for separately from prepaid-serving obligations.

# **3\. Launching Kain Tayo in a new city**

6. **Choose a local identity —** Use Kain Tayo \[City\] or another locally meaningful city name. Keep BUY 1 \+ GIVE 1 mechanics consistent.  
7. **Appoint a small coordination team —** Assign vendor onboarding, daily records, public updates, and issue resolution.  
8. **Recruit 3–5 initial vendors —** Approach carinderias, rice-meal shops, restaurants, and eligible canteens. Confirm serving size, normal price, hours, and daily reporting contact.  
9. **Agree on serving and price —** Use each vendor’s normal standard rice serving and price. The customer pays twice that price.  
10. **Sign vendor agreements —** Each vendor commits to honor prepaid credits, keep daily records, follow food-safety requirements, and settle outstanding credits before leaving.  
11. **Set up accounting —** Assign a unique Partner ID to every vendor. Record each funded and redeemed serving against that Partner ID.  
12. **Prepare signage —** Display BUY 1 \+ GIVE 1 and free-serving claim instructions. Never display recipient names.  
13. **Set a community goal —** The Dumaguete pilot used a 30-day target of 1,000 prepaid servings. Choose a realistic goal for the new city.  
14. **Publish a public dashboard —** Show funded servings, redeemed servings, outstanding balance, active vendors, and goal progress.  
15. **Evaluate after 30 days —** Review vendor participation, actual redemptions, outstanding balances, discrepancies, satisfaction, and admin costs.

# **4\. Daily procedure**

## **Funding a serving**

The customer pays for two servings at the normal price, receives one, and the vendor records one prepaid credit.

## **Claiming a free serving**

16. A person asks for a free Kain Tayo serving.  
17. The vendor checks that vendor’s available Rice Bank balance.  
18. The vendor prepares and serves one standard portion.  
19. The vendor records one redemption and reduces the balance by one.

The basic model provides one standard serving per person per visit, intended for immediate consumption. If demand exceeds supply, the local policy may prioritize students and schoolchildren, then other community members; within each group, first come, first served. Priority must never become a public identification mechanism.

# **5\. Rice Bank accounting**

**Opening balance \+ New prepaid servings − Redeemed servings \= Closing balance**

A Rice Bank balance represents prepaid servings not yet claimed. It is not cooked rice held in storage. The vendor prepares rice when someone claims it and remains responsible for honoring the credit.

Version 1 credits are not transferable between vendors. Cross-vendor redemption requires a later central clearing and reimbursement process with a unified ledger.

# **6\. Transparency**

* Total prepaid servings: credits funded by customers.  
* Total redeemed servings: servings actually provided.  
* Outstanding balance: prepaid servings still owed.  
* Active participating vendors and community-goal progress.  
* Administrative expenses disclosed separately.

Never describe a prepaid serving as delivered until it is actually redeemed. Do not collect or publish recipient names, photos, or personal stories.

# **7\. Suggested 30-day pilot**

* Days 1–5 — Preparation: finalize rules, recruit 3–5 vendors, agree prices and serving sizes, sign agreements, set up accounting, print signs, and check local compliance.  
* Days 6–10 — Public launch: activate partners, publish the goal, begin transactions, and start daily reporting. Original milestone: first 100 prepaid servings.  
* Days 11–20 — Citywide campaign: approach more vendors, student organizations, community groups, and businesses. Original milestone: 500 prepaid servings.  
* Days 21–30 — Community goal: expand participation, publish updates, and work toward the 1,000-serving goal.  
* After day 30 — Evaluation: review partners, customers, prepaid servings, redemptions, outstanding balances, satisfaction, discrepancies, and cost per redeemed serving.

# **8\. Food safety and local compliance**

Kain Tayo does not operate food-preparation facilities. Each vendor remains responsible for preparation, handling, permits, sanitation, and applicable health certificates for food handlers. Rice intended to be served hot must be handled under applicable food-safety temperature requirements. Consult the relevant local health office. Requirements vary by location; this guide is not legal or regulatory advice.

# **9\. Risk controls**

* Low participation: visible signs, simple messaging, and local outreach.  
* Demand exceeds balance: display balances and pause new credits where necessary.  
* Vendor misuse or errors: daily reconciliation, standardized records, and spot checks.  
* Unclaimed credits: carry balances forward and monitor commitments.  
* Vendor withdrawal: stop new credits and settle outstanding servings before exit.  
* Public distrust: publish aggregate reports and keep administration separate.  
* Food-safety concern: work only with vendors meeting applicable requirements.

# **12\. Future versions**

Version 1 stays simple: independent vendor Rice Banks and same-vendor redemption. Later versions may add QR partner identification, recurring sponsors, and eventually a shared Rice Bank. Cross-vendor redemption must wait until central clearing, reimbursement, and a unified ledger are in place. Each city remains locally operated and accountable.
